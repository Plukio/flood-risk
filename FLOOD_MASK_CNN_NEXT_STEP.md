# First-in-a-year masked CNN: next-step tests

**Run 24 October 2026.** Objective: improve capture of first-in-a-year Traffy report-positive 250 m cell-days from about 13% toward 15–20%, at the **same** five-fold Q4 2025 district queue. The queue selects 10% of candidate cells per day and reserves 20% of those slots for cells with no own-cell report in the preceding 365 days. There are 1,664 first-in-a-year positives and 7,209 positives overall. These are report labels, not verified wet/dry observations.

| Novel-slot ranker, same queue | First-in-a-year capture | Overall capture | Model choice |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Nearby report history | 11.946% | 44.114% | Fixed reference |
| Synthetic-center-plus-block masked CNN | 13.529% | 44.479% | Mask arm was examined on Q4 before selection |
| **Within-day rank blend, CNN weight selected on source districts** | **13.582%** | **44.492%** | Weights 0.75, 0.75, 0.5, 0.5, 0.75 across folds |
| Same blend, fixed 75% CNN weight | 13.665% | 44.511% | Diagnostic only; highest fixed-weight Q4 row |
| Hard-negative masked CNN, top 5% nearby-history pool | 6.237% | 42.796% | Source validation rejected it in all five folds |
| Adjacent/isolated CNN split, source-selected slot share | 13.469% | 44.466% | Source selected 0%, 5%, 0%, 0%, 10% of the novel reserve for isolated cells |
| **Soft daily queue-loss continuation** | 13.529% | 44.479% | Source validation retained the original checkpoint in all five folds; no trained queue-loss model selected |
| Matched pairwise continuation | 13.482% | 44.469% | Source selected one updated fold and retained the original in four |

The blend converts the CNN score and nearby-history score separately to rank percentiles among each day's first-in-a-year candidates in the district fold. It then combines those percentile ranks. The CNN checkpoints and 20% slot reserve are frozen. Each fold's blend weight was selected for first-in-a-year capture on January–September 2025 **source districts**, then evaluated on its Q4 target districts. The selected blend finds about **226** first-in-a-year positive cell-days, versus **225.12** for the CNN alone and about **198.8** for nearby history. Its gain over the CNN is negligible. A seven-day date-block interval for the selected blend minus the simple score is **−0.87 to +3.96 percentage points**; minus the CNN it is **−0.97 to +0.73 points**. These intervals omit model-selection and district uncertainty.

The hard-negative experiment kept the CNN architecture, synthetic center and random 3 × 3 masks, training years, pairwise loss, epochs, seed and queue. It changed only the comparison pool from the highest **20%** to highest **5%** of nearby-history scores, with a 25-cell floor. Despite falling training loss, novel capture dropped in every Q4 fold. Source validation chose the original model in every fold. This is evidence against that specific way of concentrating comparisons near the queue cutoff; training loss did not predict held-out capture.

The separate **adjacent/isolated split** also used saved CNNs without retraining. It scored candidates with adjacent prior-year reports using the original block-masked CNN; candidates with neither own nor adjacent prior-year reports used the earlier target-centered-mask CNN. Each fold selected how much of the *same* novel reserve to allocate to the isolated group on source validation. The selected split captured **13.469%** first-in-a-year positives, below the unsplit CNN's **13.529%**. Giving the isolated group 50% of the reserve reduced first-in-a-year capture to **9.094%**. Source validation put no isolated slots in three of five folds. The candidate split is measurable, but that CNN and allocation do not add useful capture at this budget.

## Daily queue-loss continuation, 24 October 2026

The next test started two continuation arms from the **same saved block-masked CNN**. Both trained for up to two extra epochs on the same 2023–2024 source days and the same sampled *actual* first-in-a-year positive cells, using the same optimizer and learning rate. Neither continuation arm used synthetic repeat-site positives; that shared change is why a matched pairwise continuation is necessary. The pairwise arm compared positives with random and high-nearby-history unreported cells. The queue-loss arm ran the CNN on the whole day grid and used a differentiable fixed-capacity threshold for the first-in-a-year reserve: positive cells were rewarded for moving above the smooth top-2%-of-all-candidates cutoff. A gradient and logit-shift self-check passed. Unreported cells were queue competitors, not asserted dry labels.

Each arm chose its best epoch **including epoch zero, the original checkpoint**, by January–September 2025 source-district first-in-a-year capture. The queue-loss arm chose **epoch zero in all five folds**. The pairwise arm chose epoch one only in fold 3; that fold's Q4 capture fell from **10.260% to 10.000%**. Pooled first-in-a-year Q4 capture was **13.529%** for the original and source-selected queue-loss arm, and **13.482%** for the source-selected pairwise arm. The full source-selected choice among the original and both continuations also reached **13.482%**. Thus the proposed listwise objective did not improve the masked CNN. The negative result applies to this temperature (0.10), learning rate (0.0002), and two-epoch continuation; it does not prove every possible queue-aware loss will fail.

**Current conclusion:** None of these tests reaches 15%. The numerical best is 13.665% with a fixed 75% blend, but choosing it after seeing Q4 would repeat the same selection problem as the 13.529% mask arm. The source-selected 13.582% blend is also built on a CNN mask arm that earlier work chose after examining Q4, so it is exploratory. The most defensible practical reference remains nearby history until a later untouched season or another city confirms a gain.

The training/scoring mismatch remains: nearly all synthetically masked repeat positives have adjacent prior reports, while about one quarter of genuinely first-in-a-year positives do not. The stratified queue test shows that simply diverting slots to an isolated CNN does not fix it. A stronger next training test would match synthetic examples to the **real adjacent-history distribution** while keeping the correctly masked target cell and source-only model selection. Report adjacent-history and isolated capture separately and require a gain in both before calling it promising. More original report IDs and time-matched verified water areas would make the spatial learning target substantially stronger. Increasing the reserved-slot share merely to cross 15% would change the resource allocation rather than improve the ranker.
