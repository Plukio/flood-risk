# Open data for a transferable Bangkok flood model

Checked 22 October 2026. This audit focuses on **event water labels, observation coverage, and rain history**. The existing Bangkok GISTDA holdout is mainly agricultural water, so more tiles of the same target may not improve predictions for streets or dense neighborhoods.

### Bangkok comparison on the same event

The EOS-RS Bangkok TIFF has **444,597 mapped water pixels** across its full bounds. Rasterizing the local [GISTDA 27 Sep BMA polygons](artifacts/flood_bangkapi_surface/gistda_daily_20260927_bma.geojson) on that roughly 30 m grid and applying BMA district boundaries gave:

| Within BMA | EOS-RS | GISTDA |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Mapped water area, approximate | **173 km²** | **10.6 km²** |
| Share of GISTDA mapped pixels also marked by EOS-RS | — | **95.8%** |
| Share of EOS-RS mapped BMA pixels also marked by GISTDA | **5.9%** | — |

The difference is a **disagreement between two satellite-derived proxy maps**, not proof that 173 km² of Bangkok flooded. GISTDA's polygons list `S1D_20260927_0601`, `S1D_20260927_1828`, and `rd2_20260927_1827` as sources. EOS-RS specifies the date but not a source scene ID or time. The products cannot yet serve as independent labels for each other.

In the existing **20 × 20 km Nong Chok patch**, EOS-RS maps **67.27 km²** versus **18.87 km²** in the GISTDA patch label. At the 250 m cell scale, **80.6%** of EOS-RS mapped area falls in cells with over 50% ESA WorldCover crop fraction; **6.8%** falls in cells with over 20% built fraction. EOS-RS gives somewhat more urban examples, but remains crop dominated. WorldCover is from 2021 and is used only as a land-cover approximation.

For the **2 Oct 2021 UNOSAT BMA observed area**, the local HII archive has 24 valid hourly readings at BKK002 and BKK021, totaling **20.8 mm** and **19.2 mm** on the calendar day. The UNOSAT shapefile does not give an acquisition hour, so those full-day totals cannot yet be used as *pre-observation* rain inputs or evidence that the radar saw the area after all that rain.

## How to train with non-binary evidence

Keep three concepts separate in the training table: **mapped-water fraction in each cell**, **source confidence**, and **observation status**. A cell with 30% EOS-RS blue pixels has 30% *mapped proxy coverage*, not a 30% probability of actual flooding. EOS-RS blue pixels can receive a positive loss with a source-level confidence weight; transparent pixels remain unlabeled. The 30% fraction is a **lower-bound proxy**, not a symmetric regression target that penalizes predictions above 30%. UNOSAT pixels inside a checked analysis extent, outside cloud obstruction and mapped water, can contribute **lower-confidence likely-dry** examples; there, a weighted fractional-area target may be feasible. Outside that extent and at uncertain edges, set the loss mask to zero. BMA/Traffy positives add point evidence without expanding them into invented wet circles. This supports a positive-unlabeled objective for EOS-RS and a separately weighted extent objective for UNOSAT; it does **not** by itself calibrate Bangkok street-flood probabilities.

## Next backtest worth running

1. Build event records with source scene/interval, analysis mask, cloud mask, positive fraction, land-cover class, and gauge completeness. Check every UNOSAT layer against its own metadata and clip to the actual analysis polygon; bounding-box overlap is insufficient. Treat the 2 Oct 2021 BMA UNOSAT coverage as **weak likely dry** and cross-check any BMA reports that day before using urban cells as negatives.
2. Train on UNOSAT events outside Bangkok plus the Hat Yai EOS-RS positive map, holding out whole **city–storm** pairs. Use only pre-observation rain and pre-event static imagery. Keep source-specific loss weights so a large weak proxy map does not overwhelm smaller, better labels.
3. Freeze the model and evaluate Bangkok's 27 Sep EOS-RS map as a **positive-only ranking** and BMA/Traffy event sites as a separate urban ranking. Report built-area and crop-area scores separately, alongside crop-cover and historical-report baselines. Do not use 27 Sep GISTDA as independent corroboration until scene overlap is resolved.
4. Run rain-input ablation and doubled-rain sensitivity. The latter tests whether predictions react sensibly, **not** whether the doubled-rain outcome is validated. Calibration of actual Bangkok inundation still needs time-matched observed dry ground or an independently checked observation footprint.
