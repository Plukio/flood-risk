# Getting observed flood extent for Bangkok

**Target:** a time-stamped polygon of *temporary surface water on normally dry land*, with a second polygon showing what was actually visible. The unobserved part remains **unknown**. Keep water presence separate from water depth. A BMA road report or a Traffy complaint verifies at most a site and time; expanding it to a 100 m circle or a terrain-connected area creates a proxy, not a verified boundary.

## What the open sources can establish

| Source | What it can verify | Current limit |
|---|---|---|
| [BMA road-flood reports](https://data.bangkok.go.th/dataset/frd_dds) | Positive named site, reported interval, often depth | No event polygon, exact coordinates, or sampled dry sites. |
| [GISTDA Disaster Platform flood data](https://data.go.th/dataset/flood-disaster-data) | Daily mapped-water polygons, plus 1-, 3-, 7-, and 30-day products; the STAC root includes a 2025 archive | The [Bangkok GISTDA audit](FLOOD_BANGKOK_GISTDA_EXTENT_AUDIT.md) found a working public `api_key` route and **667 BMA-intersecting polygons on 20 October 2026**, but only ten small 2025 BMA-intersecting polygons, tagged `OTH` rather than a named satellite scene. No observed-dry/coverage mask is in the downloaded GeoJSON assets. |
| [THEOS-2 example imagery](https://data.go.th/dataset/theos-2-earthquake) and [GISTDA 2 m basemap](https://data.go.th/dataset/2-gistda) | High-detail optical imagery can show water boundaries where a timely scene is clear; the basemap helps locate roads and buildings | THEOS-2 is optical, so cloud and daylight matter. The catalog's 2 m basemap is a multi-date mosaic, **not** a flood-event image. We have not found a matching THEOS-2 Bangkok flood scene in this audit. |
| [GISTDA FloodCheck camera response](https://floodcheck.gistda.or.th/cctv) | Potential future wet **and** dry observations within each working camera's visible ground area | On 22 October 2026 the response had 1,308 catalog cameras and 371 marked online, but only **21 online camera coordinates fell inside BMA districts** (nine districts; none in Bang Kapi). Six of eight sampled Bangkok HLS playlist URLs responded; a playlist response is not yet a usable flood frame. The response has no historical frame archive or observation timestamp. |
| [Sentinel-1 RTC](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-1-rtc) | Candidate larger open-area water polygons at the satellite pass time, including under cloud | The Bang Kapi 2025 audit found only **one** pass during 12 distinct BMA flood dates' reported intervals. A matching pass missed the known flooded road in the 1 km² road/radar pilot; 10 m radar is unreliable for narrow built-up streets. |
| [Sentinel-2 L2A](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-2-l2a) | Clear-sky open-water boundary, useful for long-lived larger events | No pass during a reported Bang Kapi flood interval in the 2025 audit; rain/cloud usually obscure short urban floods. |

The camera count is for the **whole FloodCheck response**, not 1,308 cameras in Bangkok. The 21-camera Bangkok figure uses the public BMA district polygons. Camera status and playlist accessibility can change. We have checked playlist HTTP responses, not archived frames, camera field of view, or image quality.

## Recommended labeling design

1. **Use the GISTDA audit to select dates.** The public example key works as `api_key`, and the STAC root includes a 2025 archive. The checked 2025 daily products give very little BMA coverage; the 20 October 2026 daily product gives substantial eastern-Bangkok positive polygons. Before modeling, obtain the scene footprint/quality mask and source methods so unobserved areas remain unknown.
2. **Start with observable surfaces.** Shortlist working cameras in Bang Sue (seven currently marked online) and other districts that also have BMA reports. Check the actual view and stream before choosing sites. Add flood-prone views and controls with no past complaints. No current Bang Kapi FloodCheck camera is marked online.
3. **Archive future storm frames at known times.** Save a baseline frame and one frame every 1–5 minutes during rain and the following few hours. Record camera ID, exact Bangkok/UTC timestamp, stream health, rain event ID, and frame hash. A camera marked online without a decodable frame produces no label.
4. **Annotate only visible ground.** Two reviewers outline water and visible dry pavement/land in each sampled frame; mask vehicles, reflections, shadows, canal water, and blocked views as unknown. Use landmarks and an orthophoto to project the visible road/ground polygon onto a map. Do not label an entire 250 m cell dry because one road view is dry.
5. **Add satellite polygons only where they are interpretable.** For long-lived open-area floods, compare same-orbit Sentinel-1 scenes and manually review water candidates against permanent-water maps, buildings, radar layover, and independent site/photo evidence. Preserve an unknown mask. A radar-darkened pixel alone is not verified water.
6. **Keep a source and confidence for every label.** Store event time, geometry, visible-area geometry, wet/dry/unknown, source frame or scene ID, reviewer agreement, geolocation uncertainty, and any BMA corroboration. Point reports remain point evidence, not polygons. Hold out whole storms and districts when testing a model.

**What we can backtest now:** BMA gives sparse positive sites, and GISTDA gives a small 2025 positive-polygon sample plus a larger 2026 eastern-Bangkok daily footprint. Sentinel-1 can add carefully reviewed larger-area candidates where a pass overlaps an event. None of these sources supplies a complete verified 2025 street-level wet/dry extent map. For a credible U-Net flood-extent target, combine suitable GISTDA polygons with a timestamped camera-frame archive, and keep uncertain urban pixels out of the training loss.
