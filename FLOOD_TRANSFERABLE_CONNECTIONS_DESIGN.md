# Transferable flood connections: design and evidence

22 October 2026. This is a design for longer-range flood relations, not a validated flood simulation. The first [uphill built-area and rain backtest](FLOOD_CITYWIDE_UPHILL_PAVED_BACKTEST.md) has now been run; its 250 m terrain-path feature did not beat the local or distance controls. The operational Bangkok benchmark is the [recent + nearby report-history queue](FLOOD_CITYWIDE_HISTORY_RANKER.md): it captures **48.17%** of Q4 2025 report-positive cell-days in the daily top 10% of cells. Report absence is not observed dryness. Q4 2025 has already been inspected in model development.

## What the existing tests say

- The [CNN–GNN flow graph](FLOOD_BANGKOK_CNN_GNN_FLOW.md) links each 250 m cell to its steepest lower neighbor, its reverse neighbor, and cells crossed successively by the same OSM canal way. On eight held-out districts, graph rank Spearman was **0.306**, versus **0.310** for its matched no-edge head. Its graphs stop at district boundaries.
- A later [shared-node canal graph](FLOOD_CITYWIDE_CONNECTED_RELATIONS.md) reaches across Bangkok. Adding connected report history captured **48.41%** of Q4 positive cell-days, versus **48.17%** for the no-rain history benchmark; the seven-day-block difference interval was **−0.14 to +0.66 percentage points**. A distance-only control did better on pre-Q4 selection data. Thus a benefit from canal connectivity has not been established.
- [Surface cover](FLOOD_BANGKOK_NEW_FEATURES.md) was the most consistent cross-district addition in a separate tree test. The [province satellite expansion](FLOOD_GISTDA_PROVINCE_EXPANSION.md) learned mostly agricultural mapped water and did not transfer to Bangkok street reports. More satellite-positive scenes alone do not repair that target mismatch.

## Candidate relations and transfer

| Relation beyond a local CNN | Candidate edges and measurable features | Transfer hypothesis | Main uncertainty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Uphill contributing area → low cell | DTM-derived downhill paths at finer resolution; path length, local drop, contributing area, upstream built/vegetated fractions, rain accumulated over that area | Runoff concentration should transfer if expressed as relative terrain and contributing-area properties | Bangkok is flat and engineered; DEM paths miss drains, walls and culverts |
| Cell → canal reach → next reach | Shared-node OSM canal topology; attachment distance, reach length, grade where trustworthy, intervening gate/pump inventory | Long-range influence follows a corridor more plausibly than a circular distance buffer | A nearby canal may not drain the cell; OSM intersections and direction do not prove hydraulic flow |
| Outlet/river → upstream canal/cells | Connected outfall/gate/pump path; inside–outside water-level difference, tide/river level, gate opening, pump rate, measurement age | A high downstream boundary can propagate backwater even when rain is unchanged | Historical paired head and operating state are absent from the [open 2022–2025 data](FLOOD_BANGKOK_SHARED_STATE_NEXT.md) |
| Barrier/crossing → adjacent paths | Roads, embankments, rail lines and underpasses as possible blockage or crossing locations | Barriers and underpasses can redirect surface flow | OSM road geometry alone does not identify kerb height, culvert capacity or a sealed crossing |

The **graph-building rules and shared edge-response function** are what can transfer. Each city needs its own graph, terrain, land cover and assets. Absolute elevation, district IDs, individual edge weights and raw complaint counts can encode local datum or reporting practice; they should not be treated as universal flood physics. Surface cover and relative terrain are available more broadly than verified drain links or time-matched operations.

## Learn a relation without turning distance into a graph

1. Build one graph across administrative boundaries. Keep distinct **surface cells, canal reaches, gates, pumps and outlets**. Attach a cell to a canal only as a *candidate* link, recording the attachment distance and confidence. Preserve unknown links rather than inventing a pipe connection. Use physically plausible direction for surface paths and separate upstream/backwater message types for canals.
2. Give each edge a shared feature vector: type, length, elevation drop per length, upstream contributing area, land-cover mix, and any known gate/pump/outfall properties. Give the node recent **hourly rain up to prediction time**, antecedent wetness, relative elevation and capacity proxies. Use water-level/head and operating state only for dates with actual timestamped observations. Include missingness and quality flags. Avoid absolute-elevation and city-ID shortcuts.
3. First test explicit graph-derived features in a small tree or linear ranking model: upstream paved-area × rain, terrain-path contributing area, connected canal distance, and outlet/backwater exposure. This shows whether the proposed paths carry signal before fitting another GNN.
4. If they do, use a local CNN/U-Net encoder for image/terrain context and a **relation-specific, time-stepped graph layer** for distant paths. A shared edge gate controls how much a message moves; constrain it to candidate edges and regularize long or uncertain links. A surface message can depend on upstream runoff and path grade; a canal/backwater message can depend on head difference and pump/gate state when observed. Decode node states to a cell map. This learns conditional transmission across cities rather than a free weight for each Bangkok edge.
5. Keep a hazard representation separate from observation heads. Train an urban report-ranking head using Traffy positives and carefully matched BMA positive sites; reporting exposure and prior complaints belong in that head. Train a satellite mapped-water head on source-specific positive polygons. Use an observed footprint/quality mask before using likely dry satellite cells. Missing complaints and transparent GISTDA pixels are **unknown**, not negative flood labels. Count independent storms/cities when weighting training, not augmented patches.

With positives alone, an edge's true hydraulic capacity or direction is usually **not identifiable**: many different networks could explain the same complaint pattern. The model can rank candidate relations, but verified wet/dry extent, timed depth, water levels, or pump logs are needed to validate physical transmission. In particular, fitting a complaint-ranking graph does not establish that doubling rainfall gives a valid inundation scenario.

## Decisive experiment

Train on earlier Bangkok storms/districts, keep later storms and whole districts out of weight fitting, and reserve an untouched future period for final claims. For cross-city transfer, hold out an **entire city that has urban flood observations**; agricultural GISTDA positives alone cannot test street-flood transfer. Match every model on event dates, candidate cells, input cutoffs and queue budget.

Compare (a) recent + nearby report history, (b) the same model with wider distance smoothing, (c) CNN/U-Net with no graph edges, (d) a graph with edge destinations shuffled within distance/type bins, (e) explicit physical-path features, and then (f) the learned graph. The shuffled control tests whether topology matters beyond edge length and model capacity. Report daily top-10% positive-site capture and district/storm variation for complaints, and a **separate** mapped-water extent score for satellite-observed events. Neither is a calibrated flood probability without observed wet and dry cells. Run rain-intensity/duration sensitivity only as an exploratory stress test until matched extent/depth events can check the scenario response.

**Decision rule:** keep a connection type only if its gain persists on unseen districts/storms, beats the equal-reach distance and shuffled-edge controls, and helps the urban target. The first **upstream built contributing area × retrospective rain** test did not clear that bar. The outlet/backwater relation should wait for credible paired level and operating records.
