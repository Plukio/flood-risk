# Satellite and Thai open-data sources for flood transfer

Checked 22 October 2026. This scan covers the six 20 × 20 km patches in the [six-region GISTDA backtest](FLOOD_GISTDA_TRANSFER.md): Nong Chok, Pathum Thani, Chachoengsao, Ayutthaya, Nakhon Sawan, and Phitsanulok. It focuses on surface cover, antecedent wetness, rain, and mapped water. A satellite-derived class is a clue about runoff, not a measurement of infiltration or drain capacity.

## What is accessible

| Source | Coverage and tested access | Best use at the 250 m cell scale | Historical-backtest limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [ESA WorldCover 2020/2021](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/esa-worldcover) | Global 10 m map assets found for all six patches through the public Planetary Computer STAC catalog. | Separate **built**, cropland, trees/grass, bare ground, wetlands, and permanent water fractions. Use 2021 as a static map before 2025–26 events. | Built class is not a measured pavement fraction; 2021 misses later construction. Already used as built/vegetated/water fractions in an earlier [Bang Kapi/Bang Khae report model](FLOOD_BANGKOK_NEW_FEATURES.md), but not yet in the six-region GISTDA CNN. |
| [Sentinel-2 L2A](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-2-l2a) | Global 10–20 m optical scenes. A January–April 2025 composite is already in the six-region experiment. In a Sep–Oct 2026 check, four Bangkok-area patches had **no** scenes with under 20% *scene-wide* cloud, versus one in Nakhon Sawan and three in Phitsanulok. | Pre-event vegetation, bare/urban spectral signals, and open-water context; cloud-masked composites can reveal land-cover changes since WorldCover. | Rainy-season clouds; scene-wide cloud percentage does not guarantee a clear cell. A built-up index is not a direct concrete or permeability measurement. |
| [Dynamic World](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/catalog/GOOGLE_DYNAMICWORLD_V1) | Global 10 m Sentinel-2-derived class probabilities, including `built`, `crops`, `bare`, and `water`, from 2015 onward. | [Sentinel-1 RTC](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/sentinel-1-rtc) | Global radar scenes found for all six patches; nearest prior passes in the scan were about **0.2–6.7 days** before label-day starts. | Pre-event radar backscatter/change as a wetness clue, especially over open land despite cloud. | Urban radar is difficult to interpret; narrow streets are often below useful resolution. Same-scene acquisitions may be part of the GISTDA label source and would leak the answer. |
| [JRC Global Surface Water](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/jrc-gsw) | Global occurrence/seasonality assets found for all patches; this catalog ends in **2020**. | Permanent or recurring water fraction and a mask around rivers, ponds, and canals. | Historical climatology, not a current flood observation. |
| [Copernicus DEM GLO-30](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/dataset/cop-dem-glo-30) | Global 30 m coverage found for the tested patches. | Broad relief outside Bangkok, alongside local relative elevation. | A surface model can include buildings and vegetation. Planetary Computer's IMERG mirror ends in 2021, so it cannot supply these 2026 events. |

The latest mosaic combines Thai and foreign imagery. | Map roofs, roads, and larger paved areas more finely than 10 m WorldCover, after a checked segmentation/validation step. | RGB map tiles are not analysis-ready multispectral scenes. Pixel acquisition dates and reuse/bulk-processing terms were not established, so do not use them as dated 2025–26 changes yet. |
| [GISTDA six-crop map](https://data.go.th/dataset/agriculture-07) | Point and nearby polygon queries returned crop records with a publicly listed key. A high-NDVI, GISTDA-wet Nong Chok cell was labeled **Rice**. | Distinguish irrigated or seasonally flooded rice from trees and urban greenery. | The service can return later revisions: a nearby crop polygon showed an October 2026 update. Use only records demonstrably dated before each event; some public keys require a referer. |
| [GISTDA drought/SMAP products](https://data.go.th/dataset/disaster-platform-drought) | Public-catalog-key STAC route returned weekly soil-moisture polygons from 2022 through **5 October 2026**. One queried polygon covered about **6.1 km²**; this is polygon area, not sensor resolution. | Regional antecedent wetness as a shared storm-state feature. | Too coarse for individual 250 m cells; no product later than 5 October was listed for the 20 October label in this scan. Direct point route required a referer. |
| [GISTDA flood polygons](https://data.go.th/dataset/flood-disaster-data) and [FloodCheck](https://data.go.th/dataset/floodcheck) | Flood polygons are already the backtest target. FloodCheck advertises WMS extents, forecasts, and 30-minute rain, but tested WMS/API routes returned 401/403. Public catalog pages themselves load. | Potential event maps and rainfall if access is later clarified. | GISTDA polygons cannot serve as an **independent** validation source for this model; FloodCheck data access is currently gated. |

The public catalog search for [THEOS-2](https://data.go.th/dataset/theos-2-earthquake) exposed specific earthquake image tiles, but did not establish an open Bangkok-wide raw archive. The accessible 2 m GISTDA basemap is therefore the concrete Thai high-resolution option found here, with the date and licensing limits above.

## How to represent paved surfaces and wetness

Use **separate classes**, not one “green” channel. Concrete, asphalt, roofs, and many compacted surfaces generally reduce infiltration; a rice field can be green and still hold floodwater. The first static cell channels should be WorldCover fractions for built, crop, tree/grass, bare, wetland, and permanent water. Keep road and building fractions from the existing OSM snapshot. For each cell, also summarize the *upstream contributing area*: a paved patch can send water downhill into a different cell. Terrain, canals, pumps, gates, and outfall water levels still determine where that runoff accumulates and how quickly it leaves.

Add time-varying information in a separate block: rain timing from gauges/IMERG, coarse SMAP wetness where available, and strictly **pre-event** Sentinel-1 change. Test Dynamic World or dated Sentinel-2 composites for post-2021 built-area change only where clear observations precede the event. The Thai 2 m RGB basemap is a promising pavement map, but its mixed acquisition dates prevent a clean historical comparison until the imagery date is established.

## Completed test and next data decision

The [matched land-cover backtest](FLOOD_GISTDA_LANDCOVER_BACKTEST.md) raised the six-region CNN's highest-ranked-10% capture from **24.0% to 29.2%** with separate WorldCover classes. Thai weekly SMAP lowered it to **26.6%**. Crucially, simply ranking by WorldCover **crop fraction** captured **32.0%**: this GISTDA target strongly reflects agricultural water. Built fraction alone captured less than the base CNN. These are rankings of positive mapped area, not flood probabilities or doubled-rain outcomes.

For the urban [Bang Kapi/Bang Khae report backtest](FLOOD_BANGKOK_SATELLITE_COVER_BACKTEST.md), existing built/vegetated/water cover features remain useful, while splitting vegetation into more satellite classes did not consistently improve held-out-district rankings. The result is exploratory because a missing report is not a verified dry cell.

The next independent data need is dated, verified urban flood **and dry** observations, or an observation footprint for the satellite product. Near-event Sentinel-1 should be tested only after matching its acquisition time to each GISTDA polygon source to prevent label leakage.
